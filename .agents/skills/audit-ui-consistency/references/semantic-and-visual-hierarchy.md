# Semantic and visual hierarchy

[User interface consistency audit](../SKILL.md)

Compare intended reading order with color, weight, spacing, indentation, borders, position, and repetition.

## Checks

- Do identifiers/timestamps outweigh their subject?
- Do repeated titles/badges/labels compete without context?
- Do connectors, indentation, wrapping, and continuations retain relationships?
- Are status, counts, and diagnostics attached to their item?
- Is selection apparent without losing semantic styling?
- Does expansion retain terminology and hierarchy?
- Do constrained views prioritize identity, outcome, and controls over metadata?

Recommend shared roles/layout where useful, not identical row counts, components, or colors for different meanings.

### Reading order

Identify each surface's:

1. **Context:** view/group heading.
2. **Primary content:** subject, target, selection.
3. **State and outcome:** progress, result, attention needed.
4. **Supporting information:** counts, timestamps, dependencies, IDs, annotations.
5. **Available actions:** controls/recovery.

This is a comparison aid, not a fixed layout; actionable failures or safety confirmations may need priority.

## Evidence

Annotate paired renders with roles/emphasis, including relevant selected, failed, narrow, and expanded states; table roles where useful. Explain what distracts from the task. Preference alone is not a defect.
